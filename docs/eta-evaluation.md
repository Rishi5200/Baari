# ETA evaluation

Generated by `com.baari.eta.Backtest` from `tokens.csv`. Do not edit by hand; rerun the backtest.

## Setup

- Data: simulated clinic days (`baari-simulator`, `sim/demo-clinic.json`), 60 days: 2026-09-22 to 2026-11-20.
- Scored: the newest 14 days (2026-11-07 to 2026-11-20). Rolling on a day only sees consultations from earlier days; the ML models are trained once on the 46 days before 2026-11-07 (time-based, never a random split).
- Predictions are logged at the production triggers: JOIN and the first time a patient is within 10, 5, 3 and 1 places. Patients who never reached a consultation (no-shows) are not scored.
- The wait is the raw projector output (no display smoothing). Default assumes 6 min per consultation; all variants use a 30 s gap between patients and no no-show allowance.
- ROLLING+CALIBRATION: Rolling once the clinic has enough history, with the range widened by the nightly calibration factor (PLAN 8.5: coverage of the last 14 days, one step of 0.1 per night toward 60 to 70%, within 1.0 to 2.5). The factor was 2.1 on the first scored day and 1.2 after the last.
- ML:LINEAR_SGD and ML:CART: Tribuo models of the log of the consultation length (PLAN 8.6) from doctor, visit type, priority, hour, weekday, place in the doctor's session, time into the session and queue length; ranges from the spread of their training errors, no calibration.
- Error = predicted minus actual, in minutes. Bias above 0 means the estimate was too long. Coverage = share of actual waits inside the shown range (the target is 60 to 70%).

## Reading

Rolling's mean wait error is 7.4 min against 8.5 min for Default (13% lower). The best ML model, ML:LINEAR_SGD, is at 7.4 min, 0% lower than Rolling, not enough to be promoted under the 5% rule (PLAN 8.6). Without calibration the shown range holds the actual wait 45% of the time (Rolling); calibration brings it to 66% without moving the estimate.

All variants lean short (negative bias, worst at JOIN). Late session starts and patients who join before the doctor arrives are not modelled, because the app stores no session timetable (ADR 0012). A better consultation-length model cannot fix that part of the error, which is why its gain on the wait is smaller than its gain on the consultation length. On the consultation length itself the models are close to Rolling too: most of the remaining error is random spread that no feature explains (the simulator draws each length from a lognormal around the doctor's median), so there is little left to learn.
